## Q1a. R223-4 (décret n° 2026-662): text and link to the energy-renovation ban [medium]
R223-4 (version in force since 11/08/2026, from décret 2026-662 of 23/07/2026) says that a professional's call answering a consumer's information request is not telephone prospecting if three cumulative conditions are met. (1) The professional can prove the request really happened. (2) The call is made within 5 business days ('jours ouvrables') after the request. (3) The call covers only the goods or services the consumer asked to be called about. The proof must be archived digitally for 3 years from the request, or longer only as needed for legal defence. Two independent search summaries of the Légifrance page say R223-4 opens with 'pour l'application du cinquième alinéa de l'article L. 223-1'. That fifth paragraph is the sector ban on calls about works or equipment for energy savings, renewable energy or adapting homes to ageing or disability. FFB and L'Officiel des métiers (Aug-Sept 2026) confirm R223-4 is the specific callback rule for these sectors, allowed 'uniquement à sa demande expresse'. So the exception is aimed at the renovation ban itself, not only at the general opt-in regime. Confidence is high for the three conditions and the 3-year archive, and medium for the exact 'cinquième alinéa' wording. Note: consent under R223-1 (general opt-in) does NOT lift the sector ban. In this sector only the R223-4 'demande' route or an ongoing contract works.

## Q1b. Lead sold or transmitted to an installer who then calls: covered by R223-4? [low]
Not by default; possibly yes under narrow conditions. This is my analysis: no official text addresses lead resale. R223-4 covers 'l'appel d'un professionnel en réponse à une demande d'information du consommateur', limited to what 'le consommateur a demandé à être appelé'. L223-1 counts calls made 'directement ou par l'intermédiaire d'un tiers agissant pour son compte' as the professional's own. R223-1 requires any consent request to name the professional and any third party acting for it. It follows that a lead sold after submission to an installer the consumer never named is not a request addressed to that installer. Its call would be prohibited prospecting, whatever generic 'partenaires' consent was given. The installer's call is defensible only if all of the following hold. (a) Before submitting, the consumer makes an explicit, separate, unticked-by-default request to be called by THAT named company (corporate name and SIREN), about a precise object (for example an air/water heat pump). (b) The call happens within 5 business days counted from the consumer's request, not from the lead purchase, so delivery must be near real-time. (c) The call stays on that object, with no upsell to insulation or solar. (d) The installer holds and archives the proof package for 3 years: exact text, page or notice version, timestamp, and request identifiers. The lead site then acts as the 'tiers' collecting the request on the installer's behalf. Remaining risks: DGCCRF has not published a position on lead generation under R223-4, older DGCCRF doctrine is hostile (see Q1c), and the decree is challenged before the Conseil d'État. Confidence is medium that an unnamed or post-hoc buyer is NOT covered, and low that naming the installer is enough.

Sources : https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/codes/article_lc/LEGIARTI000032807262; https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/codes/article_lc/LEGIARTI000054491464; https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/codes/article_lc/LEGIARTI000051830285/2026-08-11; https://www.victorisavocat.com/blog/achat-leads-consentement-demarchage-preuve-ddpp

## Q1c. DGCCRF and government positions on simulators, lead generation and callbacks (all predate R223-4) [medium]
(1) Ministerial answer to QE n° 39522 (15th legislature, about 2021): the 2020 ban 'applies strictly, including to people who entered their phone number in a contact form on the website of an energy-renovation company to be called back'. (2) DGCCRF fiche 'Conseils pour réussir la rénovation énergétique' (dated 18/11/2025): exceptions are an ongoing contract or consumers 'qui ont demandé à être rappelés'. It adds that calling back someone who left details 'sur un site ou simulateur en ligne n'est pas considéré comme conforme… même s'il y aurait consenti'. (3) DGCCRF survey of online simulators (2019, 14 sites): leads are sold to data brokers, intermediaries and installers. Internet users are not clearly told that their details go to many unnamed companies for phone prospecting. The commercial purpose is often hidden. (4) DGCCRF 'focus isolation à 1 euro' (2021): eligibility tests and simulators are used to harvest contacts for callbacks without consent, in breach of GDPR. (5) 2021 Beaumet sanction: the ban applies 'même s'il a pu donner son consentement pour être rappelé'. (6) DGCCRF consumer guidance describes the model: personal data collected through misleading eligibility-test or quote sites, then used for illegal phone prospecting. Since 11/08/2026, DGCCRF consumer guidance says calls answering an explicit request (for example a quote request) are allowed within 5 business days. I found no DGCCRF sanction in 2025-2026 naming a lead generator, and no DGCCRF doctrine on lead resale under R223-4. The decree is more recent than the simulator statement and should prevail where they conflict. Expect inspectors to test whether a simulator entry really is an explicit 'demande'.

## Q1d. Liability of the lead buyer, outsourcing and proof [medium]
Since the 2020 law, a professional that profits from illicit telephone solicitations is presumed responsible unless it shows it did not cause the breach. DGCCRF also says outsourcing calls (in France or abroad) does not shift the commissioning firm's obligations. Under the opt-in regime the burden of proof is on the professional who calls. A supplier's 'opt-in' statement on an invoice is not proof (Victoris Avocat). Buyers must hold, lead by lead, the collection page, timestamp and exact text. The Gazette du Palais (n°33, 14/10/2025, F. Binois) argued that in renovation prior consent is 'indifférent' and only the ongoing-contract exception remains. That was written before décret 2026-662 and does not cover the R223-4 request route.

## Q1e. Pending challenge to décret 2026-662, and D223-9 call windows [medium]
Trade press relayed by a law-firm blog reports that SP2C, AFRC, FVD and SNPI asked the Conseil d'État on 23/09/2026 to annul décret 2026-662. No référé-suspension was found, and the decree remains applicable. A ruling could change R223-4. D223-9 (as amended by the same decree) allows prospecting calls Monday to Friday, 10:00-13:00 and 14:00-20:00, excluding public holidays. It allows at most 4 calls or attempts per 30 days per professional. A call outside these windows is allowed only at a date and time the consumer explicitly agreed to. Whether D223-9 binds R223-4 callbacks, which are deemed not prospecting, is not settled, so it is safer to comply anyway.

Sources : https://www.victorisavocat.com/blog/recours-conseil-etat-decret-demarchage-telephonique-2026-662; https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/codes/article_lc/LEGIARTI000054491498/2026-08-11; https://www.economie.gouv.fr/dgccrf/les-fiches-pratiques/encadrement-des-jours-horaires-et-frequence-du-demarchage-telephonique-la-faq-pour-les-professionnels

## Q2. L223-8 (loi 2025-594, in force since 2 July 2025): SMS, e-mail and social networks [high]
L223-8 is in a new chapter III bis, 'Autres modes de prospection commerciale'. It bans prospecting consumers by 'message sur un service de communications interpersonnelles' (SMS, MMS, WhatsApp-type messaging), by e-mail and by 'service de réseaux sociaux en ligne' for services, equipment or works on housing aimed at energy savings, renewable energy production, or adapting homes to ageing or disability. The only exception is the performance of an ongoing contract. Any contract concluded in breach is void (L242-16-1 II). Unlike the phone ban, there is NO regulatory equivalent of R223-4 for these channels. A purely transactional reply to the consumer's own request (confirmation, appointment details, the requested quote) is arguably not 'prospection'. I found no official source confirming this, so confidence on that point is low. Promotional follow-ups, reminders, newsletters and retargeting DMs to renovation prospects are prohibited.

Sources : https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/codes/article_lc/LEGIARTI000051828335; https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/codes/section_lc/LEGITEXT000006069565/LEGISCTA000051828333; https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/codes/article_lc/LEGIARTI000051828392; https://www.anil.org/aj-loi-lutte-fraude-aides-publiqus-renovation-energetique/

## Q3. Penalties [high]
Breaches of L223-1 to L223-5, which include the phone sector ban and the opt-in rules, fall under L242-16 (unchanged since loi 2020-901). The administrative fine is at most €75,000 for a natural person and €375,000 for a legal person, and the sanction is published. Breaches of L223-8 fall under L242-16-1 (created by loi 2025-594): the same caps, and the contract is void. A contract concluded after illegal phone prospecting is also void (L223-1). DGCCRF's own pages describe the cap as up to '€75,000 par appel' and say fines add up per breach. Recent fines in the sector: NEOVIA ENVIRONNEMENT €801,020; NOVECOLOGY €440,600; H2R ENERGIES €376,080, which included failing to give the required information when collecting phone numbers on its website; Beaumet €366,930; ARTISAN CLIMAT €290,200 (11/12/2025); TECH ENR €260,900; HCS ENERGIE €90,825; ECOVERT €44,840. Criminal cases are separate: misleading practices carry up to 2 years and €300,000 (L132-2), up to 10% of turnover. The France Rénov' mention (L122-26) carries up to €15,000 / €75,000.

## Q4a. CNIL rules for transmitting or selling prospect data to partners [high]
CNIL guidance 'La prospection vers les particuliers (B to C) : quelles règles pour transmettre des données à des partenaires ?' (updated June 2026, PDF version dated 2026-09) sets five rules. (1) When the partners' prospecting requires consent (phone since 11/08/2026, e-mail and SMS), the collecting organisation must obtain consent to the transmission at collection. (2) People must be clearly told the identity of the partners for whom consent is collected and the purposes. An exhaustive, up-to-date list must be available at the moment of consent, either on the form or by link, with links to each partner's privacy policy. (3) One unticked checkbox may cover both transmission and the partners' prospecting. Accepting general terms of use is never enough. (4) Consent is valid only for the partners listed. A partner cannot pass the data on without fresh consent. (5) At first contact, and within one month at the latest, the recipient must give Art. 14 GDPR information, including the source (the name of the company that transmitted the data) and how to object or withdraw. The CNIL also requires information about changes to the list, such as new partners. Because consent covers only the partners identified at collection, a partner added later cannot use leads collected before it joined. That last point is an inference consistent with the guidance; I found no explicit sentence stating it. The GDPR Art. 13(1)(e) 'recipients' duty applies in any case. Selling leads also contradicts any notice saying data are 'ni vendues ni transmises' (see Q-repo).

Sources : https://www.cnil.fr/fr/la-prospection-vers-les-particuliers-b-c-quelles-regles-pour-transmettre-des-donnees-des-partenaires; https://cnil.fr/sites/default/files/2026-09/transmission_des_donnees_a_des_partenaires-2026.pdf; https://www.cnil.fr/fr/prospection-commerciale-par-telephone-hors-automate-dappel-quelles-sont-les-regles; https://www.cnil.fr/fr/vente-de-fichiers-clients-la-cnil-rappelle-les-regles

## Q4b. Must consent be specific per partner? [medium]
The CNIL does not require a separate checkbox for each partner. A single unticked box linked to a named, exhaustive list is accepted for e-prospecting transfers. However: (1) R223-1 (décret 2026-662) requires a phone-consent request to state the identity of the professional and of any third party acting for it, the nature of the goods or services, a validity of at most 1 year with no tacit renewal, and the right to withdraw. (2) The EDPB Guidelines 05/2020 on consent (taken over from WP29) say that where other controllers rely on the original consent, 'these organisations should all be named'; the exact paragraph was not verified. (3) In the energy-renovation sector, consent does not authorise calls anyway; only a specific request does (R223-4). For this business, then, the effective answer is yes: name the one installer who will call, per lead. Generic 'nos partenaires' consent is useless for phone calls in this sector.

Sources : https://www.cnil.fr/fr/la-prospection-vers-les-particuliers-b-c-quelles-regles-pour-transmettre-des-donnees-des-partenaires; https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/codes/article_lc/LEGIARTI000054491464; https://www.edpb.europa.eu/our-work-tools/our-documents/guidelines/guidelines-052020-consent-under-regulation-2016679_en

## Q4c. CNIL sanctions against lead-generation and data-broker firms (2022-2026) [high]
Main cases: SOLOCAL MARKETING SERVICES, €900,000 (SAN-2025-001, 15/05/2025), for prospecting with data bought from data brokers and contest sites without valid consent, partly unprovable. The injunction carried €10,000 per day and was later closed after automated and human checks of partner forms; the company stays liable for partners' consents. CALOGA, €80,000 (15/05/2025): accept buttons more prominent than the link to play, transfer to partners on 'legitimate interest' rejected, unsubscribing required e-mailing the DPO. TAGADAMEDIA, €75,000 (SAN-2023-025, 29/12/2023): a nudging form, so consent to transfer to partners was invalid. The Conseil d'État cut the fine to €50,000 on 20/05/2026 (n° 492836) for a procedural reason and upheld the injunction. A €310,000 fine (SAN-2024-003, 31/01/2024, reported as FORIOU) went to a buyer of contest-form leads used for phone prospecting: neither legitimate interest nor consent was valid, and the buyer must itself check that the prospecting conditions are met. The CNIL's 2025 review counts 10 prospecting-related sanctions, and its simplified-procedure batches include contest-form operators. I found no CNIL sanction specifically in the energy-renovation sector. The pattern is that consent is invalid when the design nudges, when it is bundled with access to the service, or when partners are not identified, and that buyers are liable.

Sources : https://www.cnil.fr/fr/sanction-de-900-000-euros-societe-solocal-marketing-services; https://www.cnil.fr/fr/cloture-injonction-solocal-marketing-services; https://www.cnil.fr/fr/sanction-de-80-000-euros-societe-caloga; https://www.cnil.fr/fr/courtiers-en-donnees-sanction-de-75-000-euros-lencontre-de-la-societe-tagadamedia

## Q5. L111-7 / D111-6 to D111-8 (platforms) and disclosure of remuneration [medium]
The loi SREN (n° 2024-449, art. 52, in force 17/02/2024) repealed the old L111-7 I definition of 'opérateur de plateforme en ligne', which had included any site 'mettant en relation plusieurs parties'. The current L111-7 obliges 'fournisseurs de place de marché en ligne ou de comparateur en ligne' to give fair, clear and transparent information on three points: terms of use and ranking or referencing rules; any contractual relationship, capital link or remuneration that influences ranking; and the advertiser's status and the parties' civil and tax rights and obligations when consumers are put in contact with professionals. Details are in D111-6 to D111-8 (décret 2024-753 of 07/07/2024). Breaches cost up to €75,000 / €375,000 (L131-4). A pure lead-capture site that passes one request to one installer, with no comparison and no contract concluded on the site, is probably neither a marketplace nor a comparator. In that case L111-7 likely does not apply strictly; this is my reading, low to medium confidence, and the post-SREN versions conflict in the sources. Regardless, three things push towards disclosing remuneration. L121-3 makes it a misleading omission not to state the real commercial intent when it is not obvious from context. The DGCCRF 2019 simulator survey criticised hidden commercial purpose. The DGCCRF 2020 home-works platform survey (14 platforms, 6 in 10 non-compliant, 15 injunctions, a €55,000 fine) criticised missing information on how the platform is paid, the price of the matching service, insurance and dispute resolution. So the site should say clearly that it is a private business paid by the installer to which the request is sent. If the site ever ranks or compares installers, D111-6/D111-7 apply in full.

## Q6a. Loi n° 2025-594 (30/06/2025, 'Cazenave'): measures relevant to intermediaries [medium]
Relevant measures. (1) Ban on SMS, e-mail and social-network prospecting in the sector (L223-8) since 2 July 2025. (2) Phone opt-in for all sectors since 11/08/2026, with the energy and adaptation ban kept and extended to adaptation works. (3) Mandatory France Rénov' message and link on any advertising or website offering energy-renovation works (L122-26, arrêté of 07/07/2026, applicable 01/10/2026, fines up to €15,000 / €75,000). (4) The professional must inform the client on a durable medium whether it holds RGE or the quality label required for aid. Before signing, it must disclose any subcontracting, identify the subcontractors and say whether they are RGE; if not, it must warn about the risk to aid eligibility. (5) Subcontracting capped at 2 tiers on works for individual homes from 01/01/2026, per trade sources; sources differ on scope, and from 2027 the invoicing firm must itself be RGE. (6) DGCCRF can suspend RGE or Mon Accompagnateur Rénov' approval. (7) Aid can be suspended for up to 3 months when serious fraud indications exist (CRPA L115-3). (8) Anah mandataires must be approved and give guarantees (décret 2025-1293 of 22/12/2025, arrêté of 29/12/2025). Exact article numbers for the RGE and subcontracting duties were not verified.

## Q6b. Requesting tax notices (avis d'imposition), numéro fiscal or FranceConnect credentials [medium]
I found no statute in loi 2025-594 or elsewhere that bans a professional from asking for an avis d'imposition. Installers and Anah mandataires legitimately need the revenu fiscal de référence (RFR) at the file stage. But official guidance is clear. economie.gouv.fr and France Rénov' say: 'Ne donnez jamais vos informations personnelles, bancaires et fiscales, lors d'un démarchage ou sur un site Internet'. They also say to beware of private aid simulators and use Simul'Aides on france-renov.gouv.fr. Since 17/08/2026 MaPrimeRénov' and MaPrimeAdapt' run through a personal France Rénov' account. Only the applicant can create it, with identity checked by FranceConnect+ or a mailed code, and the terms of use require credentials to stay confidential. Asking for FranceConnect or impots.gouv credentials therefore breaches the platform's terms and is a known identity-fraud vector (Anah has increased its identity-theft checks). Under GDPR minimisation (Art. 5(1)(c)), a lead-capture site has no need for the document itself, the numéro fiscal or the reference number of the notice. Household size plus an income bracket, as declared by the consumer, is enough to sort leads into 'bleu' (very modest) and 'jaune' (modest). The partner's request to collect the 'latest or second-latest avis' should be handled by the installer at quote or file stage, not by the lead site. There is no statutory ban, but the risk is high.

## Q6c. Bans on paying lead providers ('apport d'affaires') for MaPrimeRénov' or CEE works [low]
I found no provision in loi 2025-594, the CEE 6th-period arrêté (21/12/2025) or the Anah mandataire texts that bans paying lead providers in themselves. The only express ban on paying for referrals I found concerns lawyers. The 2025-2026 tightening works instead through other levers: the 2-tier subcontracting cap, RGE required for the invoicing firm, approval and probity rules for Anah mandataires, the presumption of liability for whoever profits from illicit solicitation, and suspension of RGE or MAR approval. The CEE trade describes apporteurs as paid per validated file or per MWh cumac. Paying for leads is therefore not illegal as such, but the leads must be lawfully obtained. Separately, from general knowledge not re-verified here, Mon Accompagnateur Rénov' must be independent of works companies. The site must not present itself as MAR, France Rénov', Anah or a mandataire.

## Q7. 'Pompe à chaleur à 1 euro' and 'installation totalement gratuite' advertising [high]
Not lawful in practice, and flagged by the authorities. (1) L121-4 19° makes it a per se misleading practice to describe a product or service as 'gratuit', 'à titre gracieux', 'sans frais' or similar if the consumer must pay anything beyond unavoidable costs. The criminal penalty is up to 2 years, €300,000 and 10% of turnover. (2) For single works ('par geste'), MaPrimeRénov' plus CEE is capped at 90% of the eligible incl.-VAT cost for very modest households, 75% for modest and 60% for intermediate (Anah guide, Sept 2026). So a minimum out-of-pocket of at least 10% / 25% / 40% always remains unless local aid applies, and total aid can never exceed 100%. A blanket '1 €' or 'gratuit' claim for heat pumps is therefore false or misleading for most households. (3) The 1-euro offers were a 2019-2021 phenomenon. The DGCCRF 'focus isolation à 1 euro' found 54% of firms non-compliant (simulators, impersonating Anah, fake RGE logos), and the scheme ended in 2021. DGCCRF and France Rénov' now list 'gratuit' and '1 €' pitches among warning signs. I found no specific 2025-2026 text banning the word 'gratuit'. The general rules plus the France Rénov' mention (L122-26) are what apply. It is fine to say that the site's service is free for the user, if true and if remuneration by installers is disclosed.

## Q-repo. Consequences for the existing ProspectEner compliance notes and code [medium]
Three changes follow. (1) CONFORMITE.md section 2 calls the R223-4 versus DGCCRF-simulator conflict an unresolved 'risque n°1'. It should now note that R223-4 appears to target the L223-1 fifth-paragraph sector ban explicitly, which supports the explicit-request callback design already built (exact sentence naming the company, channel and works, unticked box, versioned proof, 5-business-day deadline with blocking). It remains un-reviewed by DGCCRF, and the decree is challenged. (2) The current notice says contact details are 'ni vendues, ni utilisées pour… d'autres sollicitations'. Under a resale model that statement would become false, a misleading practice under L121-2/L121-3 and a breach of GDPR Art. 13. The notice, privacy policy and legal notice must say that the request is passed, for a fee, to one named installer. (3) The existing safeguards in the mise en relation flow should stay and be extended to the resale flow: named partner shown before transmission, consent tracking, a handoff recap excluding income and household data, and the server refusing when the activity is not declared. The new rules are to choose the partner before submission and to transfer within the deadline.

## Règles pratiques
- 1. Phone. Nobody calls a lead unless the consumer made an explicit request to be called by that specific company. The admin must enforce this.
- 2. Phone in the renovation sector. Only the R223-4 'demande' route works. A generic 'consentement au démarchage' (R223-1) is worthless here. Frame the form as a request, not as consent to marketing.
- 3. Pick the installer BEFORE the consumer submits. Show the installer's name, SIREN, town and RGE status, and the precise object (for example 'installation d'une pompe à chaleur air/eau', 'isolation des combles'). One company per lead.
- 4. Never re-sell or reassign a lead to a company the consumer did not name. Never add 'partenaires' after the fact. A new installer requires a new explicit request made by the consumer, never one solicited by phone, SMS or e-mail.
- 5. Suggested checkbox text, unticked, separate from the eligibility test and from the terms of use: 'Je demande à être appelé(e) par [Raison sociale], SIREN [n°], entreprise RGE, au numéro indiqué, au sujet de mon projet de [objet précis] uniquement. [Nom de l'opérateur], société privée, transmet ma demande à cette seule entreprise, qui la rémunère pour cette mise en relation.' Below the box, add 'Je peux annuler ma demande à tout moment : [lien].'
- 6. Show the eligibility result without requiring contact details, and never make the result depend on ticking the box. Refusing must be as easy as accepting. Avoid nudging button designs (lessons from the CALOGA and TAGADAMEDIA cases).
- 7. The 5-business-day clock starts at the consumer's request, not at the lead purchase. Deliver leads in real time. In the admin, block the handoff and hide 'call' actions when the deadline is close or passed; an expired lead may only wait for an inbound contact from the consumer.
- 8. The installer's call covers only the requested object. No upselling of solar or insulation to a heat-pump lead; a different object needs a new request from the consumer. Use the admin's category filters (hydraulique, PAC, isolation) to route leads to the installer whose trade matches the request.
- 9. Proof package for every lead, sent to the installer with the lead and archived 3 years by both sides: exact text ticked, notice version and hash, timestamp, page URL, chosen channel and phone number. This covers R223-4 and the burden of proof.
- 10. Calls only Monday to Friday, 10:00-13:00 and 14:00-20:00, excluding public holidays, unless the consumer picked an exact date and time. At most 4 attempts in 30 days. Stop at once and add the number to the opposition list when the person objects or withdraws, including by phone.
- 11. The operator does not call to 'qualify' unless the consumer asked the operator itself to call, within the same 5-day and same-object limits. Never call anyone to obtain consent or a request.
- 12. No SMS, e-mail, WhatsApp or social-media marketing, reminders or newsletters to renovation prospects (L223-8). Send only purely transactional messages that answer the request, with no promotional content: receipt, cancellation link, confirmation of the appointment the consumer chose.
- 13. Prefer inbound contact where possible. Show the installer's phone number or a booking link, or let the consumer pick a call slot. This is the safest route while DGCCRF has not ruled on R223-4 lead generation and the Conseil d'État case is pending.
- 14. Be transparent about the business. State plainly 'service privé et indépendant, gratuit pour vous, rémunéré par l'entreprise à laquelle votre demande est transmise' (L121-3 commercial intent). Delete any wording saying data are 'ni vendues ni transmises'.
- 15. GDPR notice and privacy policy. Name the operator (controller), the purpose, the recipient installer, the legal basis, retention periods, rights, and the right to complain to the CNIL. Keep a public, exhaustive, up-to-date list of partner installers with links to their privacy policies. Leads collected before a partner joined never go to that partner.
- 16. Contract with each installer. Use the data only for this request; no onward transfer or resale; call within 5 business days or not at all; respect D223-9 and objections; give Art. 14 information at first contact, naming the operator as the source; set retention and security; sync oppositions; allow audits.
- 17. In the same contract, the installer warrants that it holds RGE and will inform the client on a durable medium about RGE and any subcontracting before signing (loi 2025-594). For a contract signed at the client's home, it must respect the 14-day withdrawal period and the ban on payment within 7 days.
- 18. Data minimisation in the form. Ask for household size and an income bracket ('je ne sais pas' allowed, with help text pointing to the RFR line). Never collect the avis d'imposition file, the numéro fiscal, the reference of the notice, FranceConnect or impots.gouv credentials, ID documents or bank details. The installer collects documents later through the client's own France Rénov' account.
- 19. Ask only for what the installer needs to answer the request: name, phone and/or e-mail, postal code and town, heating system, emitters, surface, boiler location, year of construction, owner or tenant. Make the full address optional.
- 20. Advertising. No '1 €', 'gratuit', '0 € de reste à charge', 'aide garantie' or 'droit automatique'. Present aid as estimates subject to the écrêtement (90% / 75% / 60% incl. VAT for single works) and to Anah decisions.
- 21. No Marianne, no state colours, no claim to be France Rénov', Anah, Mon Accompagnateur Rénov' or a mandataire. Show the mandatory France Rénov' message and link (L122-26) wherever works are promoted.
- 22. Retention. Keep request proofs 3 years (R223-4). Keep prospects 3 years after collection or last contact (CNIL). Keep the opposition list at least 3 years. Anonymise automatically afterwards.
- 23. If the site ever ranks, compares or lists several installers for the consumer to choose from, add the L111-7 / D111-6 to D111-8 disclosures on ranking criteria and paid influence.
- 24. Before switching on outbound calls, get a written opinion from a consumer-law lawyer and, ideally, a position from the local DDPP on this exact flow. Watch the Conseil d'État case on décret 2026-662 and any DGCCRF update on simulators and leads.
